Grid redispatch forecast

Spain

Every day, Spain's transmission system operator (TSO), Red Eléctrica (REE), checks the day-ahead schedule against the security limits of the transmission grid. Where a line would be overloaded or voltage support would fall short, REE solves the technical constraint by redispatching generating units: upward redispatch raises a unit above its scheduled output, downward redispatch lowers it. Our model forecasts these redispatch decisions for 41 large generating units from public market and grid data. This page compares its forecasts with REE's published redispatch over 14 past delivery days.

Key terms

Called
The model forecasts that REE will redispatch the unit on that delivery day: its probability reaches the threshold in at least one hour.
Redispatched
REE actually changed the unit's day-ahead schedule, upward or downward, by at least 1.25 MWh at least once that day to solve a technical constraint.
Not called
The model's probability stayed below the threshold in every hour: no redispatch is expected for the unit that day.

Each unit on each delivery day falls into one of four outcomes: redispatched, as called (correct); redispatched, missed (the model did not call it); false alarm (called, but not redispatched); not redispatched, as expected (correct).
